# SEO Checklist for Bloggers
Here is a comprehensive breakdown of the most important SEO aspects a blog creator must handle to rank higher on search engines.

***

## Keyword Research & Search Intent

The foundation of any high-ranking blog is targeting the right keywords — especially **long-tail keywords** that match what your audience is actually searching for. More critically, you must align your content with **search intent**: is the user looking to learn, buy, or find a specific page?  Writing content that doesn't match the intent behind a query will tank your rankings regardless of how well-written the post is. [frase](https://www.frase.io/blog/5-strategies-to-choose-seo-topics-you-can-rank-for)

- Use tools like Google Keyword Planner, SEMrush, or Ahrefs to identify target keywords [tops-int](https://www.tops-int.com/blog/seo-blogging-tips)
- Incorporate **semantic keywords** (related terms and phrases) to give search engines a richer understanding of your topic [bigbusinessagency](https://bigbusinessagency.com/insights/top-strategies-to-improve-google-ranking-with-blog-content-in-2024)
- Analyze the top-ranking pages for your target keyword and aim to improve upon them [bigbusinessagency](https://bigbusinessagency.com/insights/top-strategies-to-improve-google-ranking-with-blog-content-in-2024)

***

## Content Quality & E-E-A-T

Google evaluates content through the lens of **E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness**. High-quality content must be original, well-researched, in-depth, and genuinely helpful — not just keyword-stuffed text. [aioseo](https://aioseo.com/seo-for-bloggers/)

- Write for humans first; natural, valuable writing earns rankings [midlandmarketing.co](https://midlandmarketing.co.uk/blog/seo/seo-tips-for-blogs/)
- Use data, statistics, and citations to build credibility and make content link-worthy [seranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-for-blogs/)
- Ensure articles are written or reviewed by subject matter experts, and add **author bios** to signal expertise [aioseo](https://aioseo.com/seo-for-bloggers/)
- Keep content fresh — regularly **refresh old blog posts** as search engines favor up-to-date information [aioseo](https://aioseo.com/seo-for-bloggers/)

***

## On-Page SEO Structure

Proper structure helps both readers and search engine crawlers understand your content. [writerzden](https://writerzden.com/important-on-page-seo-factors-checklist/)

- **Title tags**: Include your focus keyword; title tags are among the first signals Google uses to assess relevance [writerzden](https://writerzden.com/important-on-page-seo-factors-checklist/)
- **Header tags (H1–H6)**: Use a single H1 (your post title), H2s for main sections, and H3s for subsections; include keywords in at least 1–2 H2s [aioseo](https://aioseo.com/seo-for-bloggers/)
- **Meta descriptions**: Write compelling summaries to improve click-through rates (CTR) from search results [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)
- **URL structure**: Keep URLs short, descriptive, and keyword-rich [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)
- **Image optimization**: Compress images for speed, and always add descriptive **alt text** for accessibility and SEO [blog.hubspot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/blog-search-engine-optimization)

***

## Topical Authority & Internal Linking

Building **topic clusters** — a pillar page covering a broad subject linked to several in-depth sub-topic posts — signals to Google that your blog has genuine depth. This is one of the strongest modern SEO strategies. [aioseo](https://aioseo.com/seo-for-bloggers/)

- Interlink every new post to related existing posts using relevant anchor text [bigbusinessagency](https://bigbusinessagency.com/insights/top-strategies-to-improve-google-ranking-with-blog-content-in-2024)
- Avoid **orphan pages** (pages with no internal links pointing to them), as they are invisible to both users and crawlers [bigbusinessagency](https://bigbusinessagency.com/insights/top-strategies-to-improve-google-ranking-with-blog-content-in-2024)
- Consistent publishing within a niche builds **topical authority** over time [writerzden](https://writerzden.com/important-on-page-seo-factors-checklist/)

***

## Backlinks (Off-Page SEO)

Backlinks — links from other reputable websites pointing to your blog — remain one of Google's most powerful ranking signals. [mikekhorev](https://mikekhorev.com/seo-ranking-factors)

- Guest post on authoritative blogs in your niche to earn quality backlinks [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)
- Use the **Skyscraper Technique**: find top-ranking content, create a significantly better version, then reach out to sites linking to the original [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)
- Reclaim unlinked brand mentions by asking sites that mention you to add a hyperlink [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)

***

## Technical SEO & Page Experience

Technical performance directly impacts rankings through Google's **Core Web Vitals (CWV)** — metrics that measure page speed, interactivity, and visual stability. [seranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-for-blogs/)

| Factor | What to Do |
|---|---|
| Page Speed | Optimize images, minify CSS/JS, reduce HTTP requests  [seranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-for-blogs/) |
| Mobile-Friendliness | Ensure responsive design for all screen sizes  [bigbusinessagency](https://bigbusinessagency.com/insights/top-strategies-to-improve-google-ranking-with-blog-content-in-2024) |
| Core Web Vitals | Aim for a CWV score of 75+ using Google Search Console  [seranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-for-blogs/) |
| Bounce Rate & Dwell Time | Improve readability and content depth to keep users engaged  [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html) |

***

## Readability & Scannability

Google increasingly favors content that is easy to read and **quotable for AI-generated search summaries**. Structure your posts so they're skimmable and answer questions directly. [yoast](https://yoast.com/seo-friendly-blog-post/)

- Use short paragraphs, clear subheadings, and bold key takeaways [aioseo](https://aioseo.com/seo-for-bloggers/)
- Lead with an **answer-first structure** — state the key point early, then expand [yoast](https://yoast.com/seo-friendly-blog-post/)
- Include relevant images, videos, or infographics to boost engagement [yoast](https://yoast.com/seo-friendly-blog-post/)

***

## Content Strategy & Consistency

A documented content plan helps maintain publishing frequency and ensures you cover topics systematically. [seranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-for-blogs/)

- Build a content calendar with defined topics, formats (guides, listicles, case studies), and publishing dates [seranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-for-blogs/)
- Distribute content across social media and email newsletters to drive initial traffic and social signals [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)
- Optimize for **voice search** by targeting conversational, question-based queries [lianatech](https://www.lianatech.com/resources/blog/blog-seo-17-tips-to-make-your-blog-rank-higher-on-google-with-real-examples.html)

## Gap 1: Data, Statistics & Citations (❌ → ✅)

This is your highest-priority gap in this cluster. An AI-generated blog that cites real statistics from authoritative sources (Gov sites, .edu, industry reports) is fundamentally different — in Google's eyes — from one that makes up numbers.

### Recommended Architecture

Build a **Citation Enrichment Pipeline** as a post-generation step, not pre-generation. Here's why: you need the article's *claims* to exist first before you can find citations for them.

**Step 1 — Claim Extraction** (`/api/extract-claims`)
- After generation, send article body to Gemini with a prompt: *"Extract all factual claims, statistics, and assertions in this article that would benefit from a citation. Return as a JSON array of `{ claim, context, suggestedSearchQuery }`"*
- Example output: `{ claim: "email marketing has 4200% ROI", context: "paragraph 2", suggestedSearchQuery: "email marketing ROI statistics 2024 study" }`
- Please note that content generation should  use the manually provided data in the Engine workflow.

**Step 2 — Citation Search** (`/api/find-citations`)
- For each extracted claim, query a search API (you can use **Serper.dev** — $0.001/search) filtered to authoritative domains: `site:*.gov OR site:*.edu OR site:statista.com OR site:hubspot.com OR site:mckinsey.com`
- Return top 3 candidate URLs per claim with title and snippet

**Step 3 — Citation Selection UI**
- Show users a **"Citations Panel"** in the editor: each uncited claim is highlighted in the article body, with 2–3 suggested sources shown alongside
- User clicks to accept a citation → hyperlink is inserted automatically at the claim location
- Mark citations as `auto-suggested` vs `user-verified` — only verified ones count toward a **Citation Score** shown in the SEO dashboard

**Step 4 — Citation Score in `content-scoring.ts`**
- Add `citationScore = (verifiedCitations / totalExtractedClaims) * 100`
- Surface this alongside your existing Q-score and I-score — target 70%+ for a "well-cited" article

## Gap 2: Factual Accuracy Verification (🟡 → ✅)

Your `/api/weak-copy-detect` catches tone issues but not factual ones. AI hallucination of statistics is the single biggest E-E-A-T risk in your platform.

### Recommended Approach: Two-Layer Fact Check

**Layer 1 — Hallucination Detection (cheap, fast)**
- After generation, prompt Gemini: *"Review this article and flag any statistics, named studies, specific percentages, or proper nouns that you are not confident are factually accurate. Return `{ claim, confidence: low|medium|high, reason }`"*
- Flag `low` confidence claims visually in the editor with a ⚠️ icon — these are the highest hallucination risk
- This costs one Gemini call and catches the most egregious issues (made-up study names, fabricated percentages)

**Layer 2 — Source Verification (on-demand)**
- When a user clicks a ⚠️ flagged claim, trigger a Serper search for that specific claim
- If a real authoritative source is found, offer to convert the warning into a verified citation (feeds back into the Citation Pipeline above)
- If no source is found, suggest the user rewrite the claim more conservatively (*"Studies suggest…"* instead of *"Studies show 73% of…"*)

This two-layer approach keeps the automatic pipeline fast while giving users a clear verification workflow on demand.

### Recommended Architecture

**Freshness Score on Published Articles**

Add a `freshnessScore` calculation that runs on a scheduled job (e.g., weekly cron):

- **Age decay**: Articles older than 12 months lose points progressively — a linear decay from 100 → 60 over 24 months
- **Topic volatility**: Prompt Gemini with the article's primary keyword: *"Is this topic time-sensitive? Does it change frequently?"* — keywords like "best SEO tools 2024" decay faster than "what is SEO"
- **Competitor freshness delta**: Re-run a lightweight SERP check and compare the competitor `publishedTime` median vs your article's publish date — if competitors are 8 months newer, flag for refresh

**Refresh Workflow**

When `freshnessScore < 60`, surface a **"Refresh This Post"** CTA on the article card in the dashboard:

1. Re-run `/api/serp-extract` for the original keyword to get current competitor data
2. Diff the new competitor H2s and semantic keywords against the original article's structure — show a **"What's New"** panel: *"3 competitors now cover 'AI-powered SEO tools' — your article doesn't"*
3. Offer a **Selective Regeneration** mode: user picks which sections to regenerate vs keep (preserves any manually edited sections and existing backlinks)
4. On publish, update `publishedTime` to today and log the original date as `originalPublishDate` — this is important because Google values both freshness *and* original publication history

## Gap 1: Keyword Presence in Actual H2 Text (🟡 → ✅)

This is the easiest fix in the entire checklist — it's pure string matching that you can add directly into `content-scoring.ts` in under an hour.

### Recommended Approach

**In `content-metrics.ts`** — extract H2 text alongside H2 count (you're already counting them):

```
h2Texts: string[]  // extract innerText of all <h2> elements
```

**In `content-scoring.ts`** — add a `keywordInH2` assertion:

- Normalize focus keyword and each H2 text (lowercase, strip punctuation)
- Check: does any H2 contain the **exact focus keyword** OR a **stem match** (e.g., "SEO tools" matches "best SEO tools for beginners")?
- Check: do at least **2 H2s** contain any of the **top-3 semantic keywords** from your `liveTerms` list?
- Output: `{ focusKeywordInH2: boolean, semanticKeywordsInH2Count: number, h2KeywordScore: 0–100 }`

**In the Live SEO Editor** — extend the existing `liveTerms` highlighting: when a term has `placements: ['h2']` but is not found in any actual H2, highlight it in **red in the H2 panel** specifically, not just the general terms list. This makes the gap visible to the user inline while editing, which is more actionable than a score alone.

This single addition closes the gap completely — no external API, no Gemini call needed.

## Gap 2: Meta Description Quality Scoring (🟡 → ✅)

You currently extract and display `meta_description` but never evaluate it. A good meta description has very specific SEO characteristics that are fully rule-checkable.

### Two-Layer Scoring

**Layer 1 — Rule-Based Checks** (add to `content-scoring.ts`):

| Rule | Target | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Character length | 120–160 chars | 30% |
| Focus keyword present | Must appear once | 30% |
| Contains a CTA verb | "Learn", "Discover", "Get", "Find" etc. | 20% |
| No keyword stuffing | Keyword density < 3 occurrences | 20% |

Combine into a `metaDescriptionScore` (0–100). Surface it in the Compare tab alongside the existing `meta_description` field — show the score with a breakdown tooltip.

**Layer 2 — CTR Optimization (optional, Gemini)** — for users who want to go further, offer a one-click **"Improve Meta Description"** that prompts Gemini: *"Rewrite this meta description to be more compelling for a user searching for [keyword], keeping it under 155 characters and including a clear benefit."* This turns a passive score into an active improvement tool.

## Gap 3: URL / Slug Quality Check (🟡 → ✅)

Your `slugify()` already generates clean slugs. You just need three assertions added as a `slugScore`:

**In `article-utils.ts` or `content-scoring.ts`**:

- **Length check**: slug should be 3–6 words / under 75 characters — flag if too long (stop words bloat slugs)
- **Focus keyword present**: slug must contain the focus keyword or its primary stem
- **Stop word penalty**: count stop words ("the", "a", "and", "of", "in") — if >2, suggest a trimmed version
- **Auto-suggest**: if slug fails, generate an alternative: take the focus keyword + 1–2 modifier words, slugify, and show it as *"Suggested: `/best-seo-tools`"*

This is entirely string logic — no API call, ships in an afternoon.

## Gap 1: Topic Cluster / Pillar Strategy (❌ → ✅)

This is the most important gap in your entire SaaS for competitive SEO. Google's Helpful Content system rewards sites that demonstrate **deep, interconnected coverage of a niche** — not a collection of isolated posts. Without cluster architecture, your users are essentially building a pile of sand instead of a structure.

### Data Model Changes

Add two new fields to your article schema in Firebase:

```
articleType: 'pillar' | 'cluster' | 'standalone'
pillarId: string | null   // cluster articles reference their pillar's articleId
```

This is the minimal viable change — everything else builds on top of it.

### Cluster Mapping in `suggest-topics`

Upgrade your existing `suggest-topics` endpoint from a flat list to a **hierarchical cluster output**:

- **Input**: site niche/profile (already available)
- **Prompt Gemini**: *"For a site about [niche], generate 3 pillar topics. For each pillar, suggest 5 cluster articles that cover a specific subtopic. Each cluster article should target a long-tail keyword that supports the pillar's broad keyword."*
- **Output schema**:
```json
{
  "pillar": { "title": "SEO for Beginners", "keyword": "SEO basics" },
```

## Gap 1: Schema / JSON-LD Auto-Generation (🟡 → ✅)

This is your highest-impact gap in this cluster. You already detect `schemaPresence` on competitor pages — you just don't generate it. Schema markup directly enables **rich results** (FAQ dropdowns, How-To steps, Article dates in SERPs), which meaningfully improve CTR from search results.

### Recommended Approach: Schema Factory

Build a `schema-generator.ts` utility that runs post-generation and produces the correct schema type based on the article's content structure:

**Schema type selection logic** (deterministic, no AI needed):
- Article contains an FAQ section (`## FAQ` H2 or Q&A pattern) → generate `FAQPage` schema
- Article is a how-to guide (title contains "how to", numbered steps present) → generate `HowTo` schema
- Default for all articles → generate `Article` schema

**`Article` schema** (always generated — baseline):
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Article",
  "headline": "{{blueprint.title_tag}}",
  "description": "{{blueprint.meta_description}}",
  "author": { "@type": "Person", "name": "{{blueprint.byline}}" },
  "datePublished": "{{article.createdAt}}",
  "dateModified": "{{article.updatedAt}}"
}
```
All fields already exist in your blueprint and Firebase article record — this is purely assembly.

**`FAQPage` schema** — extract Q&A pairs from the article body using a simple regex or Gemini parse: headings that are questions + the following paragraph = one FAQ entry. Each entry maps to `mainEntity → Question → acceptedAnswer`.

**`HowTo` schema** — extract numbered list items from the article body as `HowTo → step` entries. Title becomes `name`, meta description becomes `description`.

Surface the generated schema in the Compare tab's existing schema row — replace the current boolean `present/absent` with the full JSON-LD block in a collapsible code panel, with a **"Copy Schema"** button. ## Gap 1: Answer-First Structure / Inverted Pyramid (❌ → ✅)

This is the most strategically important gap in this cluster. Google's Featured Snippet algorithm and SGE citation system both heavily favour articles that **answer the query in the first 40–60 words** before expanding into detail. Your current sectional model (`what_it_is` + `why_it_works`) prioritises depth over immediacy — which is the opposite of what snippet algorithms reward.

### What Answer-First Means Structurally

```
❌ Current pattern (depth-first):
   H1 Title
   → Background / context paragraph
   → What it is section
   → Why it matters section
   → Answer buried in paragraph 4+

✅ Answer-first (inverted pyramid):
   H1 Title
   → Direct answer paragraph (40–60 words, answers query completely)
   → Supporting detail / evidence
   → Deep-dive sections
   → FAQ / related questions
```

The direct answer paragraph must be able to stand alone — if Google pulls it as a snippet, it should make complete sense without any surrounding context.

### Recommended Approach: Two Changes

**Change 1 — Generation Prompt Update**

Add an explicit `answer_paragraph` field to your blueprint schema, generated *before* the main article body:

Prompt instruction: *"Write a direct answer paragraph of 40–60 words that completely answers the query '[focus keyword]' as if responding to a voice search. Use plain language, no jargon. This paragraph will appear immediately after the H1 and before any other content."*

Store it as `blueprint.answer_paragraph` and inject it as the article's opening block — before `what_it_is`, before any intro, as the very first content the reader sees after the title.

**Change 2 — Answer-First Score in `content-scoring.ts`**

Add a `snippetReadinessScore` to your scoring pipeline:

- **Direct answer check**: Does the first non-heading paragraph of the generated article contain the focus keyword? Is it under 60 words? Does it end with a complete sentence (not a teaser)?
- **FAQ presence**: Does the article contain an FAQ section? (`FAQPage` schema from Topic 6 feeds this naturally — they should be built together)
- **Definition detection**: For informational keywords ("what is X"), does the first paragraph contain a definitional sentence structure ("X is a…" or "X refers to…")?
- **List/table detection**: For "best of" or "how to" keywords, does the article open with or near a summary list or table? (Google heavily features these for listicle-intent queries)

Combine into `snippetReadinessScore` (0–100) shown prominently in the SEO panel with a label: *"Featured Snippet Readiness"* — this framing makes the score immediately meaningful to users who care about ranking.

### Snippet Type Routing

Different keyword intents trigger different snippet formats. Add this logic to your intent classifier from Topic 1 (they should work together):

| Intent / Keyword Pattern | Optimal Snippet Format | Answer-First Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "What is X" | Paragraph snippet | 1-sentence definition + 2-sentence expansion |
| "How to X" | Numbered list | Step 1 answer in first 60 words + numbered list |
| "Best X for Y" | Table or list | Summary recommendation table at top |
| "X vs Y" | Table | Comparison table within first H2 |
| "Why does X" | Paragraph | Causal sentence ("X happens because…") first |

Encode this routing table in your blueprint generation — when intent is classified, the prompt selects the matching answer-first pattern automatically.

Add a **"Distribute"** tab to the article detail view, appearing after an article reaches `Published` stage. This tab houses three distribution modules:

**Module 1 — Social Post Generator**

For each published article, generate platform-native social content from the article body:

| Platform | Format | Generation Prompt Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 150–300 word thought leadership post | Extract the article's core argument + 3 key insights + CTA to read |
| Twitter/X | Thread (6–8 tweets) | Answer paragraph as tweet 1, each H2 section as one tweet, final tweet = CTA |
| Instagram | Caption + 5 hashtags | Benefit-led hook + 3 bullet takeaways + branded hashtag |
| Facebook | 80–120 word post | Curiosity-gap hook + 1 key statistic + link |

Each format gets a one-click **"Generate"** button that calls Gemini with the article's `answer_paragraph`, `title_tag`, `meta_description`, and top 3 H2s as context — not the full article body (keeps costs low). Output appears in an editable text area with a **"Copy"** button.

**Module 2 — Email Newsletter Formatter**

Convert the article into a newsletter section:

- **Subject line**: generate 3 A/B test options using curiosity, benefit, and question framing
- **Preview text**: 40–90 character hook auto-populated from `answer_paragraph`
- **Body**: structured newsletter format — headline, 2-paragraph summary, 3 bullet takeaways, CTA button block
- Export as: plain HTML (paste into Mailchimp/ConvertKit) or plain text

This is a single Gemini call with a newsletter-specific formatting prompt — low cost, high perceived value for users who manage email lists.

**Module 3 — Distribution Scheduler (Phase 2)**

Integrate with **Buffer API** or **Zapier webhooks** to allow scheduled posting directly from your platform:
- User sets publish date per platform
- Your platform pushes content via Buffer to connected social accounts
- Show scheduled posts in the content calendar (links back to Topic 4's `scheduledDate` field)

Phase 2 because it requires OAuth integrations — prioritize the content generation modules first, then add scheduling once the content quality is proven.

## Gap 2: Voice Search / FAQ Block Generation (❌ → ✅)

This gap has a direct connection to Topic 7's answer-first structure and Topic 6's `FAQPage` schema — all three should be built as one integrated feature rather than three separate implementations. Here's how they connect:

```
Voice Search Intent Detection (Topic 8)
    → FAQ Block Generation (Topic 8)
        → Answer-First Paragraph (Topic 7)
            → FAQPage Schema (Topic 6)
```

### Recommended Approach: Question Intelligence Layer

**Step 1 — Conversational Query Extraction**

Upgrade your keyword pipeline with a **"People Also Ask" extraction** step:
- After SERP extraction via `/api/serp-extract`, scrape the **People Also Ask** (PAA) box for the target keyword — these are Google's own voice-search-optimised questions and the most reliable signal of conversational intent
- Store extracted PAA questions on the article project as `relatedQuestions: string[]`
- Surface them in the editor as a **"Questions to Answer"** checklist — users can tick off which ones they've covered

**Step 2 — FAQ Block Auto-Generation**

After the main article is generated, trigger a second generation pass for the FAQ block:
- **Input**: top 5 PAA questions + article body context
- **Prompt**: *"Answer each question in 40–60 words using plain, conversational language suitable for voice search. Each answer must be self-contained — it should make sense read aloud without the question."*
- Append the generated FAQ block at the end of the article as a structured `## Frequently Asked Questions` section
- Feed directly into `FAQPage` schema generation (Topic 6 Schema Factory) — no additional work needed

**Step 3 — Voice Search Scoring**

Add a `voiceSearchScore` to `content-scoring.ts`:

| Check | Weight |
|---|---|
| FAQ section present | 30% |
| Average FAQ answer length 40–60 words | 20% |
| FAQ answers contain the question's key terms | 20% |
| Article contains conversational question phrases ("How do you…", "What is…", "Why does…") | 15% |
| Answer paragraph under 60 words (Topic 7 connection) | 15% |

Show as **"Voice & Snippet Readiness"** — merging this score with Topic 7's `snippetReadinessScore` into a single combined metric, since they measure the same underlying quality: *can Google use your content directly in zero-click results?*
